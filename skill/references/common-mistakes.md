# Common PM Resume Mistakes

**Sources:** IGotAnOffer, Exponent, HustleBadger, Harvard Career Services, real PM hiring feedback.

The failure modes below are what actually gets PM resumes screened out. Fix these first.

---

## The top PM-specific mistakes

### 1. Duties instead of outcomes (the #1 killer)
"Responsible for the roadmap", "Managed the product", "Owned the backlog" describe the *job*, not the *achievement*. Every PM has a roadmap and a backlog. Replace with what changed because you were there, and by how much.

### 2. "Worked cross-functionally" as an achievement
Collaboration is the core of the job, so claiming it is not a differentiator. Never write it alone. Instead show *what you drove, with whom, to what result*: "aligned eng, design, and 3 CSM teams to cut setup from 40 to under 10 minutes".

### 3. No numbers
A PM resume with no metrics reads as a PM who does not measure their impact, a red flag for the role. If a bullet has no number and no scope, it should not be on the page. Get a metric or an honest scale statement.

### 4. No scope / no company context
"Launched Performance Management 2.0" means nothing without the context line ("$40M SaaS, 500+ enterprises, 5 product lines owned"). Missing scope makes senior work look junior.

### 5. Metric mismatch
Citing platform latency for a Growth PM role, or vanity signups for a Monetization role. Lead with the metrics the *target* role screens for (see `pm-metrics-and-impact.md`).

### 6. A weak or generic summary
"Results-driven product manager passionate about building great products." Banned filler, zero information. The summary must carry years of experience, scope, and a signature number.

### 7. Showing execution but not product sense (or vice versa)
Only shipping bullets makes a senior PM look like a project manager. Only strategy bullets with no shipped outcomes look like talk. Cover strategy, execution, impact.

### 8. Over-length / equal weight to old roles
Two most recent roles get 5-7 bullets; older roles shrink to 2-3. A 3-page resume or a 10-year-old role with 6 bullets signals poor prioritization, ironic for a PM.

## Formatting mistakes (also ATS-breaking)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Two-column / sidebar template (Canva-style) | Single column, columns scramble in ATS and slow the scan |
| Icons, photos, skill bars, ratings | Remove all, unparseable and meaningless |
| Tables used for layout | Use tab stops + spacing |
| Decorative font | Garamond, Calibri, or Arial only |
| Body below 10.5pt to cram content | Cut content instead |
| Key info in header/footer | Put everything in the body |
| Inconsistent dates ("Jan 2022", "01/2022", "Winter '22") | One format: "Jan 2022 - Present" |

## Language mistakes

- Kill: "Responsible for", "Worked on", "Helped", "Assisted with", "Involved in", "Utilized", "Various", "Successfully", "References available on request".
- Kill hollow adjectives: "results-driven", "dynamic", "passionate", "strategic thinker", "self-starter", "ninja/guru/rockstar/wizard".
- No personal pronouns. No periods ending bullets. Past tense for past roles.
- No internal codenames only insiders understand ("shipped Project Falcon"), describe what it did.

---

## The "too much information" trap

A resume is a marketing document, not an autobiography. Cut: high-school education, hobbies (unless directly relevant), a soft-skills section, an objective statement, expired/irrelevant certs, and every role older than ~10 years unless it is a marquee name or clearly relevant. Ask of each line: *would this make a recruiter more likely to call?* If not, cut it.
